Motivation

Football analysis often focuses on player quality, transfer value, or match outcomes. This project asks a different question: do champions themselves display repeated structural patterns across leagues and seasons?

H1 — Domestic Coach

Do championship-winning teams tend to work with domestic coaches?

H2 — Back-Four Preference

Do championship-winning teams tend to rely on back-four defensive systems?

H3 — Age Profile

Do champions cluster around a similar average squad age?

Data

The final dataset contains one observation per league champion and season. The project combines automated extraction from match-level and appearance-level football data with manual enrichment for coach nationality.

Methodology

01 · Team Selection

A target champion list was created for 9 leagues and 45 champion team-season observations.

02 · Matching Pipeline

Competition filtering, season filtering, fuzzy matching, and manual club ID overrides were used to align teams across sources.

03 · Feature Construction

Coach, formation, back-four rate, and average age variables were created from processed football data.

04 · Analysis

EDA, hypothesis testing, and unsupervised ML methods were applied to identify structural champion profiles.

Limitations

  • The sample size is relatively small (45 observations).
  • coach_nationality was manually enriched.
  • Team-name inconsistencies required manual matching corrections.
  • Formation labels are not fully standardized.
  • The domestic-coach result is somewhat benchmark-dependent.
  • The project is stronger on structural profiling than on causal explanation.

Conclusion

This project builds a cross-league profile of championship-winning football teams using managerial, tactical, and squad-level variables.

The final analysis suggests that domestic coaches are somewhat more common among champions, but this evidence is weak rather than decisive. In contrast, back-four systems are strongly associated with championship-winning teams, and average squad age appears to cluster in a relatively narrow and stable range.

The machine learning stage shows that champions are not all identical, but they still group into interpretable structural archetypes. Overall, the project demonstrates that title-winning teams can be studied not only through points and results, but also through a broader structural profile combining coach background, tactical identity, and squad composition.
