Motivation

Football analysis often focuses on transfers, budgets, or match outcomes. This project instead asks whether championship-winning teams themselves share a broader structural identity. By comparing champions across leagues and seasons, the aim is to reveal whether there is a recurring “champion profile.”

H1 — Domestic Coach

Do championship-winning teams tend to work with domestic coaches more often than foreign coaches?

H2 — Back-Four Preference

Do championship-winning teams tend to rely on back-four defensive systems more than alternative tactical structures?

H3 — Age Profile

Do champions cluster around a relatively stable and mature average squad age profile?

Data

The unit of analysis is one champion team in one league-season. The final dataset combines match-level, appearance-level, and player-level football data, together with manually enriched coach nationality information.

Data Sources

games.csv, appearances.csv, players.csv, and supporting raw football data files were used during construction of the final dataset.

Feature Engineering

Coach identity, dominant formation, and back-four rate were derived from team match records, while average age was computed as a minutes-weighted measure.

Manual Enrichment

Coach nationality was completed through a manual lookup file, and the domestic/foreign indicator was generated from this field.

Methodology

The workflow moved from raw data construction to exploratory analysis, hypothesis testing, and finally unsupervised learning for latent champion profiling.

1. Team Definition

A target champion list was constructed for 45 observations across leagues and seasons.

2. Matching Pipeline

Competition codes, season filters, fuzzy matching, and manual overrides were used to align teams across different sources.

3. Variable Construction

Dominant tactical structure, back-four usage, coach information, and age metrics were derived and merged into a final champion profile dataset.

4. Analysis Stage

EDA, formal statistical tests, and unsupervised ML methods were used to evaluate the research questions and uncover champion archetypes.

Limitations

Although the project successfully builds a cross-league champion profile, several limitations should be noted.

  • The final sample size is relatively small at 45 observations.
  • Coach nationality information required manual enrichment.
  • Several team names across datasets required manual matching corrections.
  • Formation labels are not fully standardized across all records.
  • The domestic-coach interpretation depends partly on the benchmark used.
  • The project is stronger in descriptive profiling than in causal explanation.

Conclusion

The final project builds a cross-league descriptive and analytical profile of championship-winning teams.

The results show that domestic coaches are somewhat more common among champions, although this evidence is best interpreted as weakly supportive rather than conclusive.

The tactical findings are much stronger: championship-winning teams overwhelmingly rely on back-four systems, and dominant formations that begin with “4” clearly dominate the sample.

The age results suggest that champions tend to cluster around a relatively stable age profile, while the unsupervised ML stage shows that champions are not identical but can still be grouped into meaningful structural archetypes.

Overall, the project demonstrates that title-winning football teams can be studied not only through points and trophies, but also through a broader structural lens involving managerial identity, tactical organization, and squad composition.
